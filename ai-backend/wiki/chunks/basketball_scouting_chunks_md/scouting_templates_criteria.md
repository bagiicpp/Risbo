## Mastering the Draft: A Comprehensive Scouting Template

Scouting basketball prospects requires a keen eye for talent, an understanding of the game’s nuances, and an appreciation for the intangibles that define greatness on the court.

Furthermore, by collecting insights from diverse sources, including player statistics, game footage analysis, and personal backgrounds, scouts can develop a comprehensive template to evaluate and project the future success of basketball prospects.

Additionally, this article will highlight five main areas to scrutinize and list the steps to create a comprehensive scouting report.

### Physical Attributes and Athleticism

- Height, weight, wingspan, and body composition provide essential baseline measurements for assessing a player’s physical profile.
- Evaluate speed, agility, and explosiveness through agility drills, sprint tests, and vertical jump measurements.
- Additionally, consider how a player’s physical attributes translate to specific positions on the court, emphasizing the importance of versatility and athleticism in today’s game.

### Skill Set Analysis

- Break down offensive and defensive skills, including shooting, ball handling, passing, rebounding, and defensive capabilities.
- Assess shooting mechanics, selection, and efficiency from various court areas, focusing on three-point shooting for perimeter players.
- Evaluate ball handling under pressure, decision-making in transition, and ability to create scoring opportunities for themselves and teammates.
- Consider defensive instincts, on-ball defense, help defense, and the ability to anticipate passing lanes and disrupt opponents’ plays.

### Basketball IQ and Mental Toughness

- Gauge a player’s basketball IQ by assessing their understanding of the game, court awareness, and ability to read and react to different situations.
- Consider intangible qualities such as leadership, work ethic, coachability, and resilience in adversity.
- Evaluate how prospects handle pressure situations, close games, and high-stakes moments.

### Performance and Production

- Review statistics, including points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, shooting percentages, and efficiency metrics, to quantify a player’s impact on the court.
- Analyze game footage to identify trends, tendencies, strengths, and areas for improvement in a prospect’s performance.
- Consider the level of competition faced and the context of individual performances, recognizing that teams must contextualize statistical output within the broader team dynamic.

- Investigate a prospect’s background, including family, upbringing, education, and previous athletic experiences, to understand their journey and motivations.
- Conduct interviews with coaches, teammates, teachers, and mentors to gain insights into players’ character, personality, and off-court behavior.
- Assess work ethic, coachability, communication skills, and cultural fit within potential teams or organizations.

### Benefits of Scouting Reports

Scouting basketball prospects requires a comprehensive approach encompassing physical attributes, skill sets, mental attributes, performance metrics, and character evaluation.

Moreover, scouts can identify and nurture the next generation of basketball talent using a template informed by detailed analysis and contextual understanding.

Each prospect brings a unique blend of strengths, weaknesses, and potential trajectories, underscoring the dynamic nature of talent evaluation in the evolving landscape of basketball.

### How Do NBA Teams Use Scouting Reports

NBA teams will employ similar scouting templates to assess prospects eligible for the 2024 NBA Draft. Concurrently, teams tailor their reports to their specific organizational philosophies and needs.

Each team will place varying emphasis on different template sections based on their strategic priorities, roster composition, and positional requirements. For example, some teams may prioritize offensive skills, placing significant value on scoring ability, shooting efficiency, and playmaking potential.

Conversely, others may focus more on defensive performance and overall defensive versatility to complement their existing lineup. Decision-making and basketball IQ will be critical factors for all teams.

Some franchises place a premium on players who demonstrate sound decision-making under pressure and the ability to adapt to different game situations. Moreover, statistical analysis, including advanced metrics and analytics, will supplement the qualitative assessments from the game film. This gives teams additional insights into prospects’ performance and potential impact at the NBA level.

Ultimately, the scouting templates will serve as valuable tools for NBA teams to identify and evaluate talent, guiding their draft decisions and player selections as they strive for future success.

**Title:** Scouting Checklist: Inside the Scouting Report

Obviously, there are as many different effective ways to scout an opponent, as there are effective coaches. Coaching philosophies, systems, personalities and styles can vary immensely. So, rather than dwell a great deal on the particulars of a report (personnel, sets, plays, defenses, presses, etc.), we’ll spend most of our time on what I’ll call, “Inside the Report”.

While video scouting has significantly changed how we scout to a large degree, the nuts and bolts of a scouting report have remained basically the same over the years. What video has afforded us is the opportunity to be more accurate because of our ability to review.

So, with that said, let’s go inside the report and ask ourselves more general questions about how we scout and how we intend to use that information.

**ESSENCE OF A TEAM**: More than anything else, Ibelieve it’s important to be able to identify for yourself and your players the essence of your opponent. It might be a word or several words (tough, unflappable, hectic, fast, nuisance, smart etc.). It might be a phrase or a sentence- “their slow (or fast) tempo is their core;” “veteran laden team that tries not to beat itself.” Obviously a team can have a couple of essence descriptions but the simpler the better-for focus.

**FOCUS:** We ended the last sentence with this word and it goes hand in hand with the essence of a team. Ithink all too often a coach can get all wrapped up in the minutiae of a report and lose focus himself and transfer that on to his players. Agood method to focus for both you and your players might be to ask yourself, “If Ionly could say 5 or 10 things about my opponent, what would they be?” You likely already do this on the pre-game blackboard at the end of the process. This way you might lead with it to get your players’ early focus.

**IDENTIFY GO TO PLAYERS and ACTION**: With this phase, you get even a little broader in your focus but you’re still pretty narrow in scope and your players-still have a manageable amount of info to process. Highlighting several key players and offensive and defensive actions will give your players a starting point. From there, you can briefly introduce the rest of the playing rotation. It’s after this phase that you can begin to lose younger, less experienced or less serious players. Everyone, up to this point, should be able to reasonably zero in on a team and retain.

**CRUNCH TIME:** This goes hand in hand with “go to” identification. Perhaps more important than what an opponent generally does is what they want to do when they have to. Teams usually fall back to a few tried and true tactics at crunch time.

**HOW YOU “SPIN” YOUR OPPONENT:** If you do start with an opponent’s essence, focus on 5-10 key attributes and discuss go to players and action, you’ve already begun evaluating and “spinning” them on behalf of your team. How you continue to spin them initially is likely to leave another lasting impression on your team. The last thing you want to do is to hype a very good team so much that your players think you fear them or gloss over a poor or mediocre team so much that you set your team up for an underachieving performance. We all know we have to “sell” not only our evaluation of the current team that we’re playing, but we also have to sell evaluations of every other team we have to play. Keeping evaluations consistent and even-handed relative to other evaluations is important for credibility.

**SCOUTING SPECTRUMS:** We’ve talked about spectrums a lot. Where are you on these spectrums? The only right answer is what works for you. Just ask yourself where you are and then if that’s where you want to be.

**US or THEM:** Do you concentrate on your getting your own team ready for a game running your stuff or do you concentrate on your opponent’s stuff. Are you going to play “your way” and make the opponent adjust to you. This is usually decided by who has the “hammer” (personnel, tradition, system, home court can all factor in).

**PERSONNEL or X’s and O’s:** Do you concentrate on your opponent’s personnel or their stuff?

**HARD COPY REPORTS or MEMORY:** Do you have player scouting reports printed or not? If this step helps players better visualize your opponent, it may be worth the effort.

**CLASSROOM WORK/VIDEO WORK or NOT:** Do you preview your opponent prior to your first prep or any time prior to the actual game. Again, will this help your players once they get to the practice floor.

**PUT IN SOMETHING NEW OR NOT:** Based on your scouting report, would you add something totally new-not just a tweak?

**DEFENSIVE QUESTIONS: ** Will you cheat off certain players to shade or double others?

Are you totally committed to the philosophy of making your opponent’s best players beat you? (Ipersonally hate it every time a lesser player scores when he gets a good look because Ihad my players cheat.

Based on your scouting report and your own team’s strengths and weaknesses, do you try to “take away” what they do (and to what extent) or do you try to “neutralize?

Do you ever discuss how you believe a game might be officiated based on those officials’ past tendencies?

Once you get to the practice floor, the next challenge is having your players accurately portray your opponent’s system. This is usually best done in a pre-practice time slot (if available). For some thoughts on using your scouting report to its fullest advantage you may also want to refer to “Practice Gimmicks to Better Simulate Game Conditions”.
