## Why is this dimension important?

Philosophy refers to the core beliefs, values and principles that guide an organisation’s overall approach and decision-making process. In the football context, an organisation’s philosophy describes its beliefs, culture and values (i.e. its identity), and more broadly, what the organisation is all about and what it stands for. It includes an understanding of what it takes to win (winning strategies, performance support and planning), now and in the future (future game, future player).

An organisation’s football philosophy significantly influences its talent identification strategy. Philosophy is the foundation on which strategies are built and executed. In fact, an organisation’s football philosophy may shape the criteria used to evaluate players and the desired style of play. Ultimately, a football philosophy guides the identification of talent, which is aligned with strategic objectives and playing style, ensuring efficiency and alignment throughout the football organisation.

To successfully create or develop a football philosophy, adequate time needs to be spent engaging with all relevant internal and external stakeholders to ensure buy-in, understanding and support. ## What is Talent Identification In Football?

Definition of talent identification: “The process of finding promising players for a football club or academy”. However, that said “Talent” is extremely complex. The fundamental challenge in talent identification is that footballing talent does not develop in a single, linear way. This is because there is a wide range of factors that affect a player’s development. We look at several of these within our portfolio.

## How does a professional football scout recognise talent?

Because talent develops in a non-linear way, professional scouts have to be able to recognise not just fully-developed talent, but potential as well. While most trained football professionals can recognise talent relatively easily, it takes greater skill and experience to recognise a player with potential.

The first step to identifying potential is to recognise what scouts refer to as “early indicators of talent.” These are key performance indicators that provide a basis for predicting which young players are likely to excel. There are lots of skills and abilities which might be seen as early indicators of talent. Different clubs—and even individual scouts—prioritise their own set of indicators. However, they all have one thing in common: they are fundamental transferable skills.
